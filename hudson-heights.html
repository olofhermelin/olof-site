Neighborhood Guide — Manhattan

Hudson Heights

Hilltop Manhattan, with river views and room to breathe.

Why Hudson Heights

Prewar space and Hudson River views, at prices the rest of Manhattan left behind.

Hudson Heights sits on a ridge at the top of Manhattan, west of Broadway, between the George Washington Bridge and Fort Tryon Park. Bennett Park, in the middle of the neighborhood, is the highest natural point on the island.

It is one of the few places in Manhattan where a prewar one- or two-bedroom co-op with real room, and sometimes a river view, is still within reach of a first-time buyer. People who move here tend to stay.

The Vibe

Quiet, green and residential. Neighbors know each other, dogs know each other, and evenings are spent in the parks. It feels more like a small town than like Manhattan.

Housing

Almost all co-ops, most built in the 1920s and 1930s. Art Deco buildings line Fort Washington Avenue. Hudson View Gardens is a Tudor-style complex from the 1920s, and Castle Village’s five towers on Cabrini Boulevard look straight over the river.

Food & Drink

Small and local. Cafés, wine bars and neighborhood restaurants cluster on West 181st and West 187th Streets, with more choice a short walk east on Broadway.

Shops & Markets

Daily errands are on 181st and 187th Streets: grocers, bakeries, pharmacies and a few independent shops. Larger stores are along Broadway.

Parks & Culture

Fort Tryon Park, with its Heather Garden and river overlooks, and the Met Cloisters inside it. Bennett Park hosts neighborhood events through the year.

Getting Around

The A express at 181st Street and 190th Street reaches Midtown in roughly half an hour. The 1 train runs along St. Nicholas Avenue to the east, and the George Washington Bridge bus terminal is at 178th Street.

Getting Oriented

From the bridge to Fort Tryon, west of Broadway.

Roughly West 181st Street in the south to Fort Tryon Park in the north, from Broadway west to the Hudson. Fort Washington Avenue is the spine; Cabrini Boulevard and Pinehurst Avenue run along the western edge.

The streets closest to the river have the views. The blocks near 181st and 187th Streets are closest to the shops and the subway elevators.

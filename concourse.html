Neighborhood Guide — The Bronx

Concourse

Art Deco grandeur on the Bronx’s great boulevard.

Why Concourse

Three-bedroom prewar co-ops, 25 minutes from Midtown, for the price of a Manhattan studio.

The Grand Concourse opened in 1909 as the Bronx’s answer to the Champs-Élysées. In the 1920s and 1930s it filled with Art Deco and Art Moderne apartment houses, many of them now protected as a historic district.

Those buildings were made for families. Sunken living rooms, real dining rooms, three and four bedrooms. For buyers who need space and a fast commute, the Concourse offers some of the best value in the city.

The Vibe

A wide, tree-lined boulevard with a strong sense of community. Long-time residents, young families, and game-day energy when the Yankees are home.

Housing

Mostly prewar co-ops with large layouts and original Art Deco lobbies. 1100 Grand Concourse is a good example. Monthly costs are often modest, and many buildings are well run by long-serving boards.

Food & Drink

Casual, local and varied. Dominican, Mexican and West African kitchens sit along 161st and 167th Streets, with sports bars and diners around the stadium.

Shops & Markets

Everyday shopping is along 161st Street. Larger stores are at the Bronx Terminal Market, a short ride south.

Arts & Culture

The Bronx Museum of the Arts, on the Concourse at 165th Street, is free. Yankee Stadium is at 161st Street. Joyce Kilmer Park and Franz Sigel Park give the boulevard its green space.

Getting Around

The B and D stop at 161st and 167th Streets, and the 4 stops at 161st. The D express reaches Midtown in about 25 minutes. Metro-North stops at Yankees–East 153rd Street.

Getting Oriented

Along the Grand Concourse, from 161st Street north.

The heart of the neighborhood runs along the Grand Concourse from about 153rd Street to 170th Street, between Jerome Avenue and the Metro-North tracks to the east.

Buildings directly on the Concourse have the grandest lobbies and layouts. The blocks nearest 161st Street are closest to both subway lines.

Buying Your First Home in NYC, Step by Step

From budget and pre-approval to the board package and closing day. The steps in order, how long each takes, and where first-time buyers get stuck.

By Olof Hermelin  ·  Douglas Elliman  ·  6 min read

Buying in New York works differently from most of the country. Attorneys run the contract, an accepted offer is not yet binding, and many buildings have a board that must approve you. Here are the steps in the order they happen.

1. Set your real budget

Start with three numbers: your down payment, your monthly comfort level, and your closing costs. Closing costs can add 1% to 4% of the price, so count them from day one.

2. Get pre-approved

A lender reviews your income, assets and credit, and gives you a pre-approval letter. Sellers expect to see it with your offer. It also tells you early if something needs fixing.

3. Decide: co-op or condo

This choice shapes your search, your down payment and your timeline. Co-ops cost less but ask more of you. Condos are more flexible and cost more.

4. Search with a plan

See enough homes to learn what your budget buys in each neighborhood. I send clients new listings the day they appear, and I check each building’s rules and finances before you fall in love with it.

5. Make an offer

Your offer goes in writing with your pre-approval and a short financial summary. In New York, an accepted offer is not binding on either side. The deal is only firm once the contract is signed.

6. Attorney review

Your attorney reads the building’s financial statements, board minutes and rules, and negotiates the contract. This usually takes one to two weeks. It is your chance to learn about planned assessments, lawsuits or repairs.

7. Sign the contract

You sign and pay a deposit, usually 10% of the price, which is held in escrow. The seller signs, and now the deal is binding.

8. Mortgage and board package

Your lender orders an appraisal and issues a loan commitment. At the same time, we put together your board package: financial statements, tax returns, reference letters and more. In a co-op, the board then interviews you. In a condo, the board issues a waiver.

9. Walk through and close

You inspect the apartment one last time, usually the day before or the morning of the closing. At the closing table you sign, pay the balance, and get your keys.

How long does it take?

Plan on two to three months from accepted offer to closing. Co-ops often take longer because of the board. A cash purchase of a condo can be faster.

The Bottom Line

The search is the fun part. The deal is won in the paperwork: a clean offer, a careful attorney, and a board package with nothing missing.
